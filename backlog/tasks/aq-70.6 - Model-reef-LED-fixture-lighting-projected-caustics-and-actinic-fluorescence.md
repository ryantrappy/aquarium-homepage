## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Real reef tanks are lit by overhead LED fixtures mixing white and royal-blue/actinic channels. Surface ripples focus that light into a sharp, bright, cellular caustic network that moves across sand, rock tops, coral and fish backs, and many coral pigments fluoresce under the blue channel. The current scene uses generic directional lights and sine-wave "caustic" bands (src/rendering/scene/underwater-optics.ts, coral-material.ts ~L370-375, sand shader in aquarium.ts) that read as soft stripes, and has no fluorescence model; the brain coral instead uses a blanket emissive texture.

Requirements:
- Lighting rig representing one or more overhead fixtures with tunable white/blue balance and intensity.
- A real caustic pattern: a custom-made caustic texture sequence (for example rendered from a rippled surface in Blender) or a real-time caustic computed from the water-surface normal field, projected from above in world XZ onto all lit materials through a shared shader chunk, attenuated and slightly blurred with depth, and also applied to fish.
- A fluorescence channel: coral assets supply a fluorescence mask; emission scales with the actinic channel intensity and feeds selective bloom. Remove blanket emissive usage.
- Animation pauses with the aquarium controls, freezes for reduced motion and page-hidden state (coordinate with AQ-14.2, which owns ambient motion acceptance, and AQ-39 for attenuation/glass).

Responsive verification uses only 1440x900 (laptop) and 402x874 CSS pixels at scale 1 (iPhone 18 Pro proxy); do not add a broader device matrix.
