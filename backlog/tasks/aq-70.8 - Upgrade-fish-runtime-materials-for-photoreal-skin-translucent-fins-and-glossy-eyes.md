## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Fish are the primary success criterion, and today they render with plain glTF MeshStandardMaterial: no wet specular layer, no iridescence, opaque fins, matte eyes. Real reef fish skin has a thin wet mucus layer with sharp highlights, guanine-based iridescence on silvery or blue/green regions for some species (for example chromis, anthias), soft pattern edges, and thin fin membranes that glow when backlit by overhead light and show fin rays. Eyes have a glossy cornea with a crisp highlight. This task is the renderer side; the Gemini modeling lane supplies maps and named parts.

Requirements:
- A documented fish material contract agreed with the modeling lane: part naming (body, each fin, eye, cornea), required maps (albedo, normal with scale/lateral-line detail, roughness, AO, thickness or fin-ray mask, iridescence mask), and supported glTF extensions (KHR_materials_clearcoat, _iridescence, _transmission, _volume, _sheen, _specular are supported by GLTFLoader).
- Fin rendering: thin-surface translucency/transmission or alpha with fin-ray masks, double-sided, stable sorting with skinned meshes and other transparent objects.
- Body: clearcoat-style wet layer, species-appropriate iridescence, tiled micro-detail normal at close range.
- Eye: cornea clearcoat highlight; avoid dead flat eyes.
- Fish cast soft shadows on rock/sand and receive caustics and environment lighting.
- Preserve the clean-pose clip player and turning overlays (AQ-58, AQ-49) and picking (alpha-picking.ts).

Responsive verification uses only 1440x900 (laptop) and 402x874 CSS pixels at scale 1 (iPhone 18 Pro proxy); do not add a broader device matrix.
