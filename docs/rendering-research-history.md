## Comparison

| Approach | Visual fidelity and animation | Runtime / interaction | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rigged, realistically textured GLB | Best potential for accurate all-angle anatomy, independent translucent fins, material response, skeletal swimming and unobscured turns. Quality depends on each species model, rig, texture and animator. | GPU skinning is efficient when meshes and textures are sized for mobile; a single scene can depth-sort and light inhabitants. Requires asset sourcing, rights checks, retopology and species-specific rigging; DOM anchors or projected targets preserve native link behavior. | Best long-term candidate **if** production-quality, licensed models and rigs can be obtained or authored. No suitable pair has yet been verified. |
| Image-textured, thick deforming surface (study) | The existing realistic image retains rich side-view details; two shaped surfaces provide approximate thickness and a continuous tailward bending wave, plus separately hinged pectorals. Narrow viewing angles reveal repeated image features, stretched geometry, and flat fin edges; image-baked shading conflicts with scene lighting. Inpainting can remove anatomical detail. This is not a photoreal 360° model. | Modest textures; the proof recomputes normals on the CPU and is not performance-optimized. Segmentation and volume tuning are manual per species; target placement and occlusion need further work. Native HTML links can stay keyboard operable. | An informative prototype only. Do not treat projected photo surfaces as proven production quality or confuse this with a finished model. |
| Animated transparent video / image sequences | Can capture convincing detail from a fixed angle, but independent turns, variable gait, depth, lighting, and interactions are limited by the footage. Multiple sequences and alpha video can consume significant bandwidth and decode capacity; sources and rights would need checking per fish. | Simple DOM or canvas targets; synchronizing clips and finding matching motion are hard. | Rejected as the sole final approach. |
| Rigid still cutout translating across the tank | Attractive side image but no real articulation or natural turns. | Cheap and accessible with DOM links. | Existing scaffold or honest fallback only; explicitly insufficient for the goal. |

Three.js [SkinnedMesh](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SkinnedMesh.html) supports bone-weighted deformation, while [AnimationMixer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/AnimationMixer.html) plays animation clips. The spike uses ordinary deformable buffer geometry rather than a skeleton to expose the approach's limits. Neither a 3D library nor a recognizable silhouette alone establishes photorealism.

## Biological references and limitations

The [Monterey Bay Aquarium clownfish reference](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/animals-a-to-z/clownfish) and [Oregon Coast Aquarium yellow tang reference](https://aquarium.org/animals/yellow-tang/) inform appearance; they are references only, not sources of redistributable photographs. The clownfish study has three black-edged white bands and a compact rounded form; the tang has a compressed yellow body, tall fins, and a pointed snout. These are recognizable traits, not a verified anatomical reconstruction. The study gives the clownfish a stronger tail wave and the tang a gentler tail wave with faster pectoral motion; the [yellow tang swimming study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12602642/) supports pectoral-driven routine locomotion, but the exact timing here is an artistic approximation.

The two front/back surfaces repeat the same side image. When viewed head-on, eyes and markings stretch and repeat, and the body is not watertight; thin fins have no physical volume. There is no real reef depth or occlusion, light matching, species-aware path, calibrated acceleration, startle behavior, or production service binding. At extreme orbit angles the links remain center-projected placeholders rather than fitting the silhouette. The proof does not establish mobile performance with a full school. These defects make it unsuitable for the main aquarium without further asset and motion work. A production candidate must demonstrate those behaviors and be reviewed against references before integration.

## Licensed two-species anatomy and rig inspection (in progress)

The separately downloaded CC BY models above provide a better all-angle starting point. Blender imported the clownfish as one 1,605-triangle mesh with three packed 2,048² images, no bones or clips; a neutral front view shows two distinct eyes and modeled mouth rather than a side image stretched over the face. The pectorals, body and other features are a single connected mesh after coincident-vertex merging. The tang imported as one 1,184-triangle mesh with one packed 1,024² image, no bones or clips. Its neutral front view is compressed and has distinct eyes and a modeled mouth, but the source did not show discernible independent pectoral fins. Both still need visual assessment against live-fish references, including materials, fin attachments and swimming.

`rig-candidate.py` makes a separate local derivative of each: it normalizes their orientations, adds a six-segment axial armature and a provisional two-second procedural cycle, and applies simple spatial weights. The clown's fin weights are deliberately approximate. Because the tang source lacks visible pectorals, the script adds two thin closed-volume translucent membranes with geometric rays and individual bones; these are newly authored experimental geometry, not supplied by JayNmeGaming and not proven biologically accurate. The tang body does not use pectoral weights. Root's first visual review found improved all-angle volume but an oval-looking tang fin patch, a coarse dorsal outline, an opaque clown pectoral and a need for a stable head with a tailward wave. Iterations since then smoothed the tang one subdivision, moved the fin hinge toward the lower gill and reshaped its fan against a reference, added a static operculum edge, increased clown fin weighting and added tentative translucency to the outer fin faces, and reduced head amplitude. Root subsequently accepted the recognizable fan and closed frontal faces **only as a technical asset-pipeline baseline**. The clown still shows angular inner fin triangles, and fin/material fidelity remains AQ-12/AQ-40 work. The source color textures retain baked shading that material tuning alone cannot remove. Neither source has separately articulated eyes, gills or mouth, and the rig does not implement species-aware paths or turns. Blender printed divide/overflow/invalid matrix warnings during export and a multi-image sampler warning for the clown. Warning-as-error instrumentation traced the former to the exporter's normal-transform matrix multiplication; instrumenting that call showed an identity matrix and entirely finite normal inputs between −1 and 1. A scan of all float accessors in both exported GLBs found no non-finite values or absolute values above 1,000, and output normal lengths were approximately one. These checks narrow the warning's context but cannot establish correctness or eliminate visual defects. Keep the derivatives local while production fidelity remains unresolved.

The checked-in derivatives `candidate-real-clown.glb` and `candidate-real-tang.glb` contain the two experimental swim clips. The controlled three-engine entry page `engine-study.html` loads those same files and the reef LOD. Recreate the fish exports with Blender **5.0.1** and exact source GLBs matching the hashes in `asset-manifest.json`:

```sh
blender -b --python src/rendering/proof/rig-candidate.py -- /path/to/clownfish.glb src/rendering/proof/candidate-real-clown.glb clown
blender -b --python src/rendering/proof/rig-candidate.py -- /path/to/yellow-tang.glb src/rendering/proof/candidate-real-tang.glb tang
```

`inspect-model.py` inventories model components, and `render-model-views.py` renders neutral views; raw imported topology edge counts can reflect glTF UV/normal seam splits. Sources remain external; derivative hashes, byte sizes, mesh/image counts and validation summaries are in `asset-manifest.json`. This is not a finished fish pair, habitat, performance test, or engine selection.

**Interim assessment:** Mesh2Motion is worth adapting as a starting tool for rig placement and clip experimentation, but its default shark fit is not suitable unchanged. ## Structural validation of current licensed derivatives

The official [Khronos glTF Validator](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator), npm package `gltf-validator@2.0.0-dev.3.10`, reports **zero errors** for all three checked-in controlled-study derivatives. The clown has three warnings: two primitives use a normal map without exported tangents, allowing different runtime-generated tangent spaces, and one skinned mesh is not a root node. The tang has five warnings, all for non-root skinned meshes. The reef has no errors, warnings or informational messages. These results establish structural validity within this validator's checks, not correct anatomy, skin deformation, material response, or photographic realism. Missing clown tangents remain a cross-engine material comparison concern. Both the rig and meshes are transformed together in the viewer rather than moving a skinned mesh node alone.

Reproduce without changing application dependencies: unpack the official npm tarball in a temporary folder with `npm pack gltf-validator@2.0.0-dev.3.10 --pack-destination /tmp`, extract it, then run `node src/rendering/proof/validate-models.mjs /path/to/unpacked/package/index.js src/rendering/proof/candidate-real-clown.glb src/rendering/proof/candidate-real-tang.glb src/rendering/proof/candidate-reef.glb > /tmp/aq-validation.json`. The script includes each input's SHA-256, full issues and validator version, and exits unsuccessfully if there are validation errors. The checked-in `src/rendering/proof/validation-report.json` preserves the current complete report.

## Reef study derivative and reproducibility

`src/rendering/proof/candidate-reef.glb` is the same reef file loaded by the Three.js, Babylon.js and PlayCanvas fixtures. It is a controlled-engine study derivative under CC BY 4.0, not a production habitat. The source GLB is 244 MB and remains external; its exact hash and official listing are in `ASSET_NOTICES.md`. The checked-in derivative is 9,936,488 bytes, SHA-256 `b8ad404d0caca115f22153d7d97642415412f4527391a7285da7fba190001a86`. It has 16 materials, the original `UVMap`, 99,985 triangles in the exported GLB (99,999 in the pre-export Blender checkpoint), and 16 used atlases: fifteen 1024×1024 and one 1024×512. The post-export mesh has 111,035 vertices after material/UV seam splits.

To regenerate, obtain the exact licensed source through its official listing, verify its SHA-256 against `ASSET_NOTICES.md`, then run Blender 5.0.1: `blender -b --python src/rendering/proof/prepare-reef.py -- /path/to/underwater_coral_reef_photogrametry_3d_scan.glb src/rendering/proof/candidate-reef.glb --triangles 100000 --texture-size 1024`. The recipe joins material chunks, welds coincident positions while preserving loop UVs, decimates, fits the maximum axis to two units, proportionally downsamples only used material images and exports JPEG quality 90. Tangent export is disabled because none of the reef materials has a normal texture. The script prints output triangles, used texture dimensions, bytes and SHA-256. The compact derivative and report are sufficient to open the study from a clean checkout; the external original is needed only to reproduce it.

The simplification error is a one-way sampled nearest-surface measure: 5,002 regularly-strided source vertices are queried against the simplified mesh BVH before normalization, and distances are divided by the source's maximum-axis span. RMS is 0.00037619739331, p95 is 0.000748424619 and maximum is 0.003248955 of that span. It is not a symmetric Hausdorff bound, a guarantee about unsampled points, or a texture-quality measurement. The exact values and repair record are in `asset-manifest.json`.

The error values were measured on the 99,999-triangle Blender checkpoint; they are not an error bound on the exported GLB. The exported file contains 99,985 triangles, a 14-triangle decrease during glTF export. Source, checkpoint and GLB counts are recorded separately in the manifest.

The initial GLB validator error was `ACCESSOR_VECTOR3_NON_UNIT` at `/meshes/0/primitives/0/attributes/TANGENT`, accessor indices 12412–12414, value length zero. The saved geometry checkpoint had no zero-normal or zero-area polygons; the validator also reported that all emitted tangents were unused because no material had a normal texture. The derivative was re-exported from that checkpoint with tangents omitted. No geometry, normals, UVs, materials or textures were altered during repair. Khronos validation then reported zero errors, warnings and infos. Root reviewed front and oblique offline renders and accepted this scan for technical engine comparison only. Its baked dark shading, open/cut underside and substrate footprint remain visible; corrected-reef browser review at 402×874 and 1440×900 is pending Mac unlock.

To build the portable controlled fixture from only checked-in files, run `npx vite build --config src/rendering/proof/vite.config.js --outDir /tmp/aq-engine-study-build --emptyOutDir`. The config explicitly builds `engine-study.html`; its `new URL(..., import.meta.url)` GLB references make Vite emit the three hashed GLB files. The production build was verified to contain only `engine-study.html`, exactly three GLBs, and byte-for-byte/hash-identical copies of all three source derivatives. Browser checks with this production build remain pending.

### Direct-light/color normalization

The initial hemispheric/ambient setups were not equivalent and produced a misleading brightness difference. The current fixture uses two identical white directional sources, from normalized `(1,4,6)` and `(-3,-1,4)` with intensities 3 and 1, no ambient/environment light, and no tone mapping. The gray control uses sRGB `#808080`, metallic 0, roughness 0.9 (PlayCanvas gloss 0.1). Babylon receives the linearized albedo factor; PlayCanvas directional emission is aligned along its actual local −Y axis. Default camera/output gamma differences and BRDF implementations remain relevant.

At 1440×900, a 40×30 center patch of the control (x 350–389, y 495–524) measured **0.23974 mean linear RGB** in both Three.js and Babylon.js. PlayCanvas measured 0.79789 at exposure 1; setting its scene exposure to **0.30** measured **0.23717**, about 1.1% lower. Upper and lower control patches then agreed within about 5%. Measurements decode the screenshots' sRGB channels with the standard piecewise sRGB transfer function before averaging. `/tmp/aq-calibration-three.jpg`, `/tmp/aq-calibration-babylon.jpg`, and `/tmp/aq-calibration-playcanvas-matched.jpg` show the normalized fixture. This is empirical normalization for this fixture, **not a universal engine conversion**, identical BRDF proof, IBL calibration, or an engine-fidelity ranking. Baked fish shading and absent clown tangent attributes remain asset limitations shared by every engine.

The displayed 180-frame median/p95 **frame intervals** are not GPU render times or controlled device benchmarks; tab scheduling, shader compilation and development-module loading affect them. Final measurements with the same corrected reef are still pending. TypeScript checking and an isolated Vite production build pass. A crisp real 3D reef, comparable environment lighting, occlusion-aware service links, navigation, underwater optics, SSR lifecycle and production performance remain untested. No renderer migration is performed.

### Browser-only lifecycle and behavior boundary (implementation plan)

The existing TanStack Start route server-renders `AquariumScene` and already reads reduced motion inside a React effect. A selected 3D renderer should follow that boundary: server-render stable canvas/fallback markup; dynamically import and construct the engine only inside the mounted component's effect, after its canvas ref exists. Do not import the current standalone HTML study into a route: its top-level DOM access is intentionally browser-only. All three engines were bundled by Vite with TypeScript; this is not yet an SSR/remount test.

The effect must own one scene, render loop, resize observer, media-query subscription and input-listener set. Its cleanup cancels further asynchronous work, stops the render loop, removes listeners/observers, disposes scene meshes/materials/textures and releases the graphics context/device. If a pending GLB load resolves after unmount, dispose that result instead of installing it in an abandoned scene. React development remounts must not create a second live loop or retain GPU assets. Three requires explicit owned-resource traversal/disposal; Babylon provides scene/engine disposal, and PlayCanvas application/asset disposal, but neither removes the need to handle pending loads and user-created listeners. The isolated studies stop/destroy their engine on pagehide; they do not prove this complete component lifecycle.

Keep the AQ-35 profiles and AQ-44 per-instance state/paths outside engine-specific GLB clips. Feed each renderer poses and gait parameters; retain stable fish/service IDs in picking metadata. The coral mesh and future AQ-34 habitat anchors supply occluders and clearance queries; surface navmesh support is not a solution to volumetric swimming. Production links must use current visible geometry and nearest-occluder results, retain native anchor and keyboard behavior plus the Services modal, and dispatch AQ-13's one-fish startle without delaying navigation. The study only reports target names. This lifecycle/navigation contract is planned, not implemented or validated by the standalone probe.
