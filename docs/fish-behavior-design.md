This note digests the proposed shared fish-behavior model and records biological constraints for future implementation. The sourced target remains the 15 species in the [reef fish roster](reef-fish-roster.md). Eels and cleaner wrasses discussed below are optional future roster candidates; they are not current supported species or implicit additions. This is a planning brief, not an implementation or a production-asset license.

## Corrections to the proposals

| Proposal | Source-based correction | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Give every fish the same `WANDER / INTERACT / GRAZE / PERCH / FLEE` loop. | These are useful state labels, but they do not describe every species and should not force every fish through each state. The current roster has burrowers, cave hoverers, host-associated fish, schooling fish, substrate foragers, and open-water swimmers. A state such as `PERCH` can mean a blenny holding to a rock, while a firefish hovers above a sand burrow and a watchman goby keeps watch at its entrance. [FAO fire dartfish guide](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/CDrom/bobp/cd1/Bobp/Publns/MAG/021.pdf), [Australian Museum: yellow shrimpgoby](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/yellow-shrimpgoby-cryptocentrus-cinctus/), [Bali Wildlife: tailspot blenny](https://baliwildlife.com/encyclopedia/marine-life/ray-finned-fishes/blennies/tailspot-blenny-ecsenius-stigmatura/) | Keep a small shared state machine, but let each stable-ID profile enable only biologically supported states and define their species-specific meaning. `GRAZE` can represent algae grazing for a tang or prey searching for a dragonet; don't treat them as one motion. |
| Hard-tether ocellaris clownfish to 10–15 cm from an anemone. | *Amphiprion ocellaris* uses host anemones, not branching coral. Documented natural hosts include *Stichodactyla gigantea*, *S. mertensii*, and *Heteractis magnifica*. A clownfish establishes an association with a host; sources do not establish a universal 10–15 cm movement radius. [Peer-reviewed host-choice study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6850181/), [Aquarium of the Pacific](https://www.aquariumofpacific.org/onlinelearningcenter/species/clown_anemonefish) | Give the clownfish a compatible host-anemone anchor and a small, soft home region that discourages long excursions. Treat leash distance as an adjustable animation parameter, not a biological constant. Allow normal movement into and among tentacles; the anemone is a soft habitat interaction, not a solid reef obstacle. |
| Use branching *Acropora* as the clownfish host. | *Acropora* is not an anemone and is not a documented host for this species. Branching coral may be a refuge for other fish (for example, blue tang juveniles/subadults), but it cannot stand in for a clownfish's host anemone. [Host-choice study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6850181/), [FishBase/WoRMS: blue tang ecology](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?id=219676&p=taxdetails) | Represent anemones and hard coral as different typed habitat locations with a compatibility check. Only compatible species may attach a host behavior to the anemone anchor. |
| Make every tang swim with generic tail wag. | Yellow tangs primarily use pectoral fins during routine swimming, transition to body-and-caudal-fin swimming at higher speeds, and use burst-and-coast at still higher effort. Blue tang morphology also supports pectoral-fin flapping; the available comparative paper describes genus-level fin form and should be treated as morphological evidence, not a species-specific kinematic recording. [Yellow tang swimming study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12602642/), [Acanthurid fin-shape study](https://academic.oup.com/evolut/article/80/7/1542/8675045) | Model steady pectoral-fin locomotion for yellow tangs; transition to body-caudal undulation only for higher-effort travel and reserve burst-and-coast for a distinct event. Keep blue tang's pectoral-flapping motion as a morphology-based animation choice; don't animate a continuously wagging tail as its cruise gait. |
| Make all gobies and blennies perch on rock. | Habitat and movement differ within these groups. *Cryptocentrus cinctus* is a sandy burrow sentry that shares its burrow with an alpheid shrimp; *Nemateleotris magnifica* hovers over a burrow and retreats into it; a tailspot blenny is associated with sheltered coral habitat and can be represented close to rock, but should not inherit the goby's sand-burrow behavior. [Australian Museum: yellow shrimpgoby](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/yellow-shrimpgoby-cryptocentrus-cinctus/), [FAO fire dartfish guide](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/CDrom/bobp/cd1/Bobp/Publns/MAG/021.pdf), [FishBase: tailspot blenny ecology](https://nrm-fishbaselinux01.nrm.se/Ecology/Ecsenius_stigmatura) | Select habitat anchors from each species profile: `sandBurrowEntrance` for watchman goby and firefish; `rockPerch`/`grazingSurface` as a design-supported anchor for tailspot blenny. Keep documented behavior and inferred staging separate. |
| Add moray and garden eel behavior now, with one generic eel spline and an 80% buried-time rule. | Neither eel is in the 15-species target. Eel-like swimming does use traveling waves along the body, but morays and garden eels have different habitats and daily movement: morays shelter in reef crevices and their visible mouth opening is respiration; garden eels hold tail-first in sandy burrows, sway while feeding on passing plankton, and retreat tail-first. A public aquarium's spotted garden eel profile says more than half its body is usually buried; this does not establish an 80% rule for all eels or situations. [American eel swimming study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15107438/), [Florida Aquarium: garden eel](https://www.flaquarium.org/explore/habitats/shorelines/garden-eel/), [California Academy of Sciences: spotted garden eel](https://www.calacademy.org/learn-explore/creature-closeups/spotted-garden-eel), [Florida Aquarium: green moray](https://www.flaquarium.org/explore/habitats/coral-reefs/moray-eel/) | If separately approved as roster additions, design separate morphologies and anchors. A garden eel stays rooted in a sand opening, sways its exposed portion, and withdraws tail-first. Represent its hidden body with a sand occlusion/buried section or emergence parameter; do not squash or scale the whole mesh to imitate burial. A moray remains mostly in a crevice with restrained head movement and traveling body waves only during swimming. An open/close mouth loop means ventilation, not an aggression cue. |
| Add cleaner-client interaction to the roster and set it to 2–3 seconds. | The current 15 contain no obligate cleaner wrasse; a six-line wrasse is not a substitute. Bluestreak cleaner wrasses (*Labroides dimidiatus*) use cleaning stations, approach and inspect clients, and clients may solicit cleaning by holding still and spreading fins / opening mouth. Cleaner and client terminate interactions variably; sources record interaction duration as an observation, not one universal 2–3 second interval. [Review of cleaner-wrasse behavior](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1055790315002729), [field study of cleaner/client response](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5539144/) | Keep cleaning as an optional future species/interactions task requiring a roster decision and compatible client states. If used as a visual beat, make duration configurable and allow clean completion, refusal, client departure, and recovery. Any short timing chosen for pacing is a UI tuning value, not a biological fact. |
| Flee into a cave whenever a user approaches or clicks any fish. | Escape responses are linked to threat cues; a user click is an application input, not a natural predator cue. Different target species have different refuges: host anemone, sand burrow, crevice, or branching coral (and some have no documented cave retreat). [Reef-fish looming-stimulus study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42585182/) | Trigger `FLEE` only from an explicit event, then route to a profile-compatible refuge and recover after a bounded interval. Treat an authored click/startle-and-recover effect as a separate UI interaction and keep service opening immediate; do not assume proximity is danger or send every species into a generic cave. |

## Target-roster behavior anchors

Use the [15 sourced species briefs](reef-fish-roster.md) as the primary profile content. These examples show how to turn their evidence into profile data without claiming every proposed movement is measured field behavior:

- `ocellaris-clownfish`: compatible host anemone and a bounded home patch; permit natural tentacle overlap. Avoid *Acropora* host references and a hard-coded universal tether length.
- `yellow-tang`: routine pectoral-fin cruise, higher-effort body-caudal transitions, and a distinct burst-and-coast state; keep the body comparatively steady during routine travel. [Yellow tang swimming study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12602642/)
- `blue-tang`: adult open reef cruising / aggregation zone; optional juvenile/subadult branch-cover retreat. The branch retreat evidence is stage-specific. [FishBase/WoRMS](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?id=219676&p=taxdetails)
- `royal-gramma`: hover near cave/overhang, rotate to the local rock plane, and allow belly-up swimming beneath ledges. [Aquarium La Rochelle](https://www.aquarium-larochelle.com/en/species-encyclopedia/royal-gramma/)
- `firefish` and `yellow-watchman-goby`: separate sand-burrow anchors and retreat behaviors; the firefish hovers above its entrance, while the watchman goby keeps lookout at its entrance. [FAO](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/CDrom/bobp/cd1/Bobp/Publns/MAG/021.pdf), [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/yellow-shrimpgoby-cryptocentrus-cinctus/)
- `mandarin-dragonet`: low, stop-start foraging near sand/rubble and rock rather than a generic full-tank cruise; courtship is a separate, occasional state. [OATA](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-marine-fish/how-to-look-after-dragonets-mandarin-fish/)
- `blue-green-chromis` and `lyretail-anthias`: distinguish a coordinated school above branching coral from a looser group over a coral outcrop. School/aggregation alignment should have small per-fish offsets rather than identical positions.
- `six-line-wrasse`, `flame-angelfish`, `copperband-butterflyfish`, and `tailspot-blenny`: use species-specific search/grazing/perching zones near reef structure; do not funnel every fish through the same caves. For tailspot blenny, rock perching is a useful design interpretation of its sheltered coral-reef association, not a hard movement rule. [FishBase](https://nrm-fishbaselinux01.nrm.se/Ecology/Ecsenius_stigmatura)
- `mccoskers-flasher-wrasse`: separate ordinary patrol from occasional male fin-flaring courtship display; don't make the display the idle locomotion loop. [Maldives fish guide](https://www.mrc.gov.mv/assets/Uploads/fishes-of-the-maldives.pdf)

## Lightweight behavior architecture

Keep species knowledge in immutable, stable-ID-keyed profile data, and per-fish variation in runtime state. A profile can declare:

- Movement class and visible gait (pectoral fin drive, body-caudal undulation, near-stationary hover, or substrate-constrained movement), plus an adult/sex/life-stage variant where it changes the silhouette or behavior.
- Preferred depth/zone and compatible semantic anchors such as `hostAnemone`, `rockCrevice`, `sandBurrowEntrance`, `branchingCoralRefuge`, `grazingSurface`, `foragingPatch`, or `schoolCenter`.
- Enabled states, state-specific animation cues, valid targets, event triggers, and a recovery destination. Omit states unsupported for that species.

The shared engine can use a small finite-state machine: `TRAVEL`, `HOLD`, `FORAGE`, `DISPLAY`, `FLEE`, and `RECOVER`. These labels describe implementation states, not a claim that every fish performs each behavior. Use transitions with explicit predicates and per-instance timers; use profile data to configure state availability and target compatibility. A generic plugin framework or external behavior-tree package is unnecessary for the current fixed roster and simple interaction graph.

For an initial prototype:

1. Give every fish an independent seed, home/foraging anchors, phase offsets, and state timers so individuals do not synchronize.
2. Advance movement and timers with elapsed simulation time, not one behavior step per rendered frame. Bound any catch-up/substeps after a long frame; pause or suspend simulation time when the scene is paused or hidden.
3. Route through typed habitat anchors and account for 3D reef obstacles and occlusion. Never send a fish through solid rock to satisfy a target; use a compatible nearby refuge or choose a different route. Only soft anemone tentacles may overlap their resident clownfish.
4. Put durations, leash widths, hover offsets, school spread, and startle recovery in tuning data. Label these as visual tuning parameters, not universal biological measurements. Prefer a soft home-region weight over a sharp distance snap.
5. Keep visual and interaction effects subordinate to navigation, reduced motion, and accessibility. A service click should launch immediately; any startle or recovery is nonblocking.

The configured services and the 15-species planned target are separate concerns: use stable catalog IDs as profile keys, but don't implement or display species just because a behavior archetype exists. Eels and cleaner wrasses require separately sourced roster/asset work before they can enter the supported species set.
